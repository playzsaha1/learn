CARS

Cars with internal-combustion engines (ICE) work by converting fuel—usually gasoline—into rotational energy that powers the wheels through a process called four-stroke combustion. Inside the engine's cylinders, a mixture of air and fuel is drawn in, compressed, and then ignited by a spark plug, causing a tiny, controlled explosion. This explosion drives pistons down, turning the crankshaft, which transforms the linear motion into rotational motion . This rotation is passed through a transmission—which manages speed and power—and ultimately to the wheels, allowing the vehicle to move. The entire process happens thousands of times per minute while driving.
